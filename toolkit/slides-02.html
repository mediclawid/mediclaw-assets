6 things every doctor and hospital should know about how complaints are handled under Law 17/2023 on Health.

01
Know the standard

A bad outcome is not automatically malpractice

A doctor's duty is generally one of best effort, not a guaranteed result. Liability turns on whether professional standards and SOPs were breached, not on the outcome alone.

Explain risks clearly and record them in the informed consent. It is often your strongest evidence later.

Law 17/2023 on Health · Civil Code, Art. 1365

02
Discipline comes first

A professional council reviews the case

  • The Health Law sets up a disciplinary council to enforce professional discipline
  • It examines whether a doctor or health worker breached professional standards
  • Its findings and recommendation shape any later criminal or civil case
Law 17/2023, Art. 304–309

03
Criminal reports

Police need a council recommendation first

Before investigating a doctor for an alleged crime in practice, investigators must first request a recommendation from the council. It must be issued within 14 working days.

Summoned by investigators? Involve counsel early and ask whether the recommendation has been requested.

Law 17/2023, Art. 308(1), (3), (7)

04
Civil claims

Civil lawsuits follow the same gate

A claim for compensation against a doctor or health worker also requires a council recommendation. The request is filed by the doctor or their proxy.

The Constitutional Court has upheld this requirement as professional "gatekeeping", not a bar to justice.

Law 17/2023, Art. 308(2), (4) · Const. Court Decision 156/PUU-XXII/2024

05
Resolve, don't escalate

Disputes go to settlement outside court first

When a patient suffers loss from an alleged professional error, the dispute must first be resolved through alternative dispute resolution outside court, such as mediation.

Build a clear complaint-handling and mediation SOP. Most disputes start with poor communication.

Law 17/2023, Art. 310

06
Criminal exposure

Negligence can still be a crime

  • Negligence causing serious injury: up to 3 years' prison or a fine up to Rp250 million
  • Negligence causing death: up to 5 years' prison or a fine up to Rp500 million
  • Prevention: SOPs, complete medical records, valid consent
Law 17/2023, Art. 440
