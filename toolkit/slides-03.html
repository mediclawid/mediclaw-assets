6 rules every hospital and clinic must follow under Law 17/2023, MoH Reg. 24/2022 and the PDP Law.

01
Go electronic

Electronic medical records are mandatory

Every healthcare facility must run Electronic Medical Records (EMR). The transition deadline was 31 December 2023.

Still on paper or a hybrid system? Treat it as a compliance gap, not an IT project for "later".

MoH Reg. 24/2022, Art. 3(1) & Art. 45

02
Ownership

The document is yours. So is the liability.

The medical record document belongs to the facility, which is responsible for its loss, damage, forgery or unauthorised use.

The information in it is about the patient, who keeps the right to access information on their own health.

MoH Reg. 24/2022, Art. 25 · Law 17/2023 on Health

03
Retention

Keep records for at least 25 years

EMR must be stored for a minimum of 25 years from the patient's last visit. Data still in use may be excluded from destruction.

Switching EMR vendors? Make the contract guarantee full data migration and export, so nothing is lost when the old system is shut down.

MoH Reg. 24/2022, Art. 39
4/8
04
Interoperability

Your EMR must connect to the MoH platform

Stored EMR must be connected and interoperable with the Ministry of Health's health data interoperability platform (SATUSEHAT).

Ask your vendor for proof of integration and check that data standards match before go-live.

MoH Reg. 24/2022, Art. 21
5/8
05
Disclosure

Opening a record needs a legal basis

Disclosure is with the patient's consent, or without it only for listed purposes, e.g.:

  • Law-enforcement requests
  • Ethics or disciplinary enforcement, medical audit
  • Outbreaks, health emergencies, disasters
MoH Reg. 24/2022, Art. 33 & 35 · Law 17/2023

06
Data protection

Health data is "specific" personal data

  • Data breach? Notify patients and the PDP agency in writing within 3×24 hours
  • Large-scale processing of specific data requires a Data Protection Officer
  • High-risk processing requires a DPIA
Law 27/2022 (PDP), Art. 4(2), 34, 46(1), 53(1)
