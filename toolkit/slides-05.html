6 informed consent rules every doctor and hospital must follow under Law 17/2023 on Health.

01
The baseline

Every service needs consent

Each individual health service by a medical or health worker must have the patient's consent, given after an adequate explanation.

Consent is a process, not a form. A signature without a real explanation does not protect you.

Law 17/2023, Art. 293(1)–(2)

02
The explanation

7 things you must explain

  • Diagnosis and indication
  • The procedure and its purpose
  • Risks and possible complications
  • Alternatives and their risks
  • Risk of not doing it, and prognosis
Law 17/2023, Art. 293(3)

03
Written or oral

Invasive or high-risk? Get it in writing

Consent may be oral, but it must be written before any invasive and/or high-risk procedure.

The form is signed by the patient or representative and witnessed by one medical or health worker.

Law 17/2023, Art. 293(4), (5) & (8)

04
Who consents

The patient decides first

Consent comes from the patient. Only if the patient is not competent may a representative give it.

Note in the medical record why the patient was not competent and who signed on their behalf.

Law 17/2023, Art. 293(6)–(7)
5/8
05
Emergencies

The emergency exception is narrow

No consent is needed only when all three apply:

  • The patient is not competent
  • Emergency action is needed
  • No one is available to consent

Act in the patient's best interest, then explain once they or a representative can hear it.

Law 17/2023, Art. 293(9)–(11)

06
Informed refusal

Patients may say no

Patients have the right to an adequate explanation and to accept or refuse medical action, except action to prevent infectious disease or handle an outbreak.

When a patient refuses, explain the risks and document the refusal in writing.

Law 17/2023, Art. 276(b) & (d)
