Is your clinic’s accreditation still valid?

6 accreditation rules every clinic, lab and private practice should know under MoH Reg. 34/2022.

01
Who must comply

Accreditation is mandatory

Every clinic, health laboratory, blood transfusion unit, Puskesmas and independent doctor’s or dentist’s practice must be accredited.

No BPJS contract? Still mandatory. Hospitals are accredited under their own separate standards.

MoH Reg. 34/2022, Art. 3(1); Law 17/2023 on Health

02
New facilities

The 2-year deadline

Your first accreditation is due no later than 2 years after you start operating under your first business licence.

Start preparing in year one: quality indicators, SOPs and patient safety incident reporting all form part of the preparation.

MoH Reg. 34/2022, Art. 3(2) & Art. 14(2)

03
Validity

Valid for 5 years, then again

The electronic accreditation certificate is valid for 5 years. After that, the facility must be re-accredited, periodically, every 5 years.

Put the expiry date of your certificate in the management calendar, not just in a file.

MoH Reg. 34/2022, Art. 4 & Art. 20(3)
4/8
04
Re-accreditation

Apply 3 months before expiry

Re-accreditation must happen before the status expires. The application is due at the latest 3 months before the expiry date.

Survey teams and slots take time to book. Plan to start 6 to 12 months ahead.

MoH Reg. 34/2022, Art. 25(1)–(2)
5/8
05
Not accredited?

There is a remedial survey

If the survey result is “not accredited”, a remedial survey may be carried out within 3 to 6 months of that decision.

Treat the survey findings as your action plan. Fix them early, not in the last month.

MoH Reg. 34/2022, Art. 21

06
Keeping your status

Status can be revised or revoked

The status can be adjusted or revoked if services fall short of the national quality indicators, if acts endanger patient safety, or if validation shows a mismatch.

Never display a status you do not hold: deliberately showing a false accreditation status is sanctionable.

MoH Reg. 34/2022, Art. 31(1) & Art. 33
