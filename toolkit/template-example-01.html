01
Know the definition

Telemedicine is a clinical service, not just an app

The Health Law defines telemedicine as the delivery of clinical services through telecommunication and digital technology.

It is one part of telehealth, which also covers non-clinical services such as health information and public health.

Law 17/2023, Art. 1(21)–(22)

02
Who may provide it

Only healthcare facilities can deliver telemedicine

  • Hospitals
  • Puskesmas (community health centres)
  • Clinics
  • Independent practices of doctors & health workers
  • Health laboratories
  • Pharmacies
GR 28/2024 on the Implementation of Law 17/2023

03
Platforms & partners

Tech platforms are partners, not providers

A facility may run its own telemedicine system or partner with a registered Electronic System Operator (PSE).

The partnership agreement should clearly allocate roles, data handling and liability. Professional responsibility for clinical decisions stays with the treating doctor.

Law 17/2023 · GR 28/2024

04
Your doctors

Every doctor online still needs a valid STR and SIP

Telemedicine services must be delivered by medical and health personnel who are registered (STR) and licensed to practise (SIP) in Indonesia.

Check credentials before onboarding doctors to your platform, and monitor expiry dates.

Law 17/2023, Art. 172(4) · GR 28/2024

05
Records & data

Health data is "specific" personal data

  • Telemedicine encounters must be documented in electronic medical records
  • Health data gets stricter protection under the PDP Law
  • You need a lawful basis, strong security and a breach-response plan
MoH Reg. 24/2022 on Medical Records · Law 27/2022 on Personal Data Protection

06
Business set-up

Structure the business before you launch

  • Choose the right legal entity and KBLI codes in OSS
  • Register the platform as a PSE with Komdigi
  • Keep clinical services inside a licensed facility
  • Review foreign-ownership rules if investors are offshore
Risk-based licensing (OSS-RBA) · GR 28/2024
